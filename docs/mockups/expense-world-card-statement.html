A card's reconciliation as one statement

A card is two accounts, Signature PEN and Signature USD, sharing the statement code SIG. Its monthly statement is two reconciliations, one per currency, over exactly the same days: the statement's two halves. Every line of both halves gets a place on the paper statement (the statement-order screen, drawn in expense-world-statement-order.html), and once the statement is completed each line shows its statement line, such as SIG-202610-004.

These screens show a card's statement as one statement: listed together, created together, opened together, completed and reverted together. They assume two changes the engine coders already have: a reconciliation names its other half and whether the statement is in order (the app reads that instead of working it out), and completing or reverting either half does both halves in one write. An account with no statement code, such as Sueldo Soles, keeps today's screens unchanged.

The accounts, lines and amounts are invented. Card balances are signed as the paper shows them: negative is what you owe. The engine's words are its real ones; words the app adds are listed under Pick as new.

1 · The Reconciliations browse — ⏎ on Signature PEN (or Signature USD: the same list opens)

Today ⏎ on an account lists that account's reconciliations. On a card account it lists the card's statements, both halves together, so opening either card account shows the same list and both carry the ▸. Four months: Julio has only a soles half; Agosto is completed; in Septiembre the soles half was completed before the dollar half was made, so the statement is half completed (the trap screen 4c guides you out of); Octubre is the open month. In order means every line of both halves has its own place, which the engine says; it is what lets c go through.

2 · Creating a card statement — n on the card's list

The engine asks the app to guide you so a card's two halves are always created together, over the same days. A half made later over the days of a completed one cannot complete until that one is reverted and both halves are put in order again (screen 4c). The engine refuses two halves over different days in the same month.

4c · A half joined to a completed one — Septiembre 2026: the soles half completed before the dollar half was made

The engine accepts a dollar half created over the days of a completed soles half. Its lines have no place, and no order can be sent while either half is completed, so the way out is: u reverts the soles half (the dollar half is already a draft), o puts both halves in order, c completes both. The completed half's lines show without tick boxes. The engine's sentence here leaves out the part about shared places, since none is shared; the engine names only what is wrong, so its exact wording for this case is its own.

Pick:
  • 1 · The browse: A, one row per statement, or B, a heading with its two halves under it. Recommended: A: the panel shows as many months as today, and the statement's own screen has each half's figures.
  • 2 · Creating: A, one form makes both halves, or B, today's form, then the other half is offered. Recommended: A: you type the days once, so the halves always match, which is what the engine asks for.
  • 3 · The statement's screen: A, both halves on one screen, or B, one half at a time with tab. Recommended: A: it reads like the paper statement, both currencies together.
  • 3 · The questions: “ok” or what to change in Complete the statement? and Revert to draft? (both halves, only y goes ahead).
  • 4 · Not in order: A, c sends and the engine refuses with a line naming o, or B, c asks first. Recommended: A: the header already warns, and the engine stays the one that refuses.
  • 4c · A half joined to a completed one: A, the way out always on the header, or B, only after the refusal. Recommended: A: it guides you before you hit the wall, as the engine asks.
  • Still open: what d deletes on a card statement: both halves, or nothing until you ask for each half. And whether u (rows in no batch) on a card lists both accounts' rows. Not drawn; say which you expect.
