Engine uptake — what you will see change

Drawn 2026-09-23 for docs/engine-uptake-plan.md. On that day the engine changed how every list is read. Most of the catch-up is invisible, and this file draws only the parts you will see: six items, M1–M6.

Everything in yellow is new, and struck red is going away. Everything else is on screen today, drawn from the code. Rows and amounts are made up.

What you are asked

  • Three real choices: M2 (reconciliations: load each account's batches when you land on it, or all at the start), M3 (ledger after a terminal resize: keep your place, or back to page 1), M6 (real scrolling in the u pane: now or later).
  • Three to confirm: M1 (Home loses Activity and Rates, which you already decided), M4 (the u pane and the LOG bar peek look the same as today), M5 (the typed commands' footers).

M2 · Reconciliations: when each account's batches load

Why this changes: the engine no longer hands out every account's batches in one read. It now answers one account at a time. Today the screen reads everything once and switches the lower pane instantly as you move. Two ways to keep that working:

M3 · The ledger after you resize the terminal

Why this changes: the ledger used to jump to "row 41". Now it can only say "the page after this one". Pages are cut at the size the terminal had when you walked them, so a resize leaves the pages you already walked cut at the old size. You are on page 3 of a filtered ledger, and you make the window taller:

M4 · The u pane and the LOG bar peek look the same as today

Why this is here at all: both read differently under the hood, and the old way would now show wrong rows with no error. After the fix they look as they do today. The one new rule (your decision, 2026-09-23) is that the row count and the rows load together, and if the count cannot be read, the whole pane fails. It never shows rows next to a guessed number.

M5 · The typed commands: what the footers say

Only the line under each table changes, apart from reconcile list, which now needs an account. Tables and columns stay as they are.

transactions list · page by bookmark, not by number
zsh
$ expense transactions list --account-id <id> --limit 20
  … 20 rows …

(showing 20 of 214; pass --offset 20 --limit 20 for more)
(more: pass --after eyJrIjpbIjIwMjYtMDktMDFUMDU6MDA6MDAr… with the same filters)

$ expense transactions list --account-id <id> --sort date_asc     # oldest first, new flag

No total: counting would cost a second read on every list, and --json users can call the count themselves. On the last page there is no footer. --offset is gone, and --from/--to now include the whole last day (this also fixes the "--to drops the last day" defect).

accounts / categories / hashtags list · everything, always
zsh
$ expense hashtags list
  … all 56 rows …
(showing 20 of 56; pass --offset 20 --limit 20 for more)

No footer, and no --limit/--offset. Today the list stops at 20 and six tags are missing wherever something forgot to page. That bug goes away.

The footer always says which stretch you are looking at, because an empty table otherwise looks like "no history". rates list --date X still works. activity list --resource-id X also shows only the last 30 days, per your decision; its help text will say so.

$ expense reconcile list --account-id <id>
  … that account's batches, statement order, same columns as today …

$ expense reconcile get <id>   deleted: its rows are `transactions list --reconciliation-id <id>`

M6 · Optional: real scrolling in the u pane

The pane stops at the 40 oldest because reaching the oldest rows used to cost two reads and a trick. That reason is gone, so pgdn could now load the next 40 oldest, the same way the ledger pages.

What I need you to pick

M2 · A or B?

I recommend A. It opens as fast as today, costs one read per account you actually look at, and does not grow with the account list.

M3 · A or B?

I recommend B. Resizes are rare, and every page after one stays exact. A keeps your place but can repeat or skip rows on the way back up.

M6 · yes, no, or later?

I recommend later. It is new behaviour, not catch-up, and the uptake is big enough already.

M1, M4, M5 · confirm, or tell me what to change

M1 is your earlier decision drawn out. M4 is "the same as today". M5 is the footer wording, and reconcile list needing --account-id.
