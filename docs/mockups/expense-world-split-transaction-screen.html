A screen for split transactions

Your idea (2026-10-23): “split transactions should have their own menus, since they are a bit more complex.” A split transaction is one bank line in several parts. Today you change it in three different places, and each lets you change something the other two don't. This page shows those three places as they are today, then two ways to give a split transaction one screen of its own.

Every screen is drawn from the app's real code. The transaction is invented: Tottus Jockey Plaza -100.00 on Sueldo Soles, 2026-10-11, operation 47391026, split into -60.00 COMIDA (#casa, “verduras”) and -40.00 HOGAR (“lejía y papel”). Use the button at the top right for a light terminal.

Your question · “shouldn't we be unable to edit transactions in a reconciliation, or only some columns?”

Short answer: “only some columns” is already the rule, and the engine keeps it. What changes with where the transaction sits:

In no reconciliation (the rows Reconciliations ▸ an account ▸ u lists as “no batch”): everything can change — date, title, operation, amount, account, category, hashtags, note. It can be split again and deleted.

In a draft reconciliation: everything can still change, from the ledger. (A reconciliation's own screen has no ⏎: there you only tick rows in and out with space.) Moving it to another account takes it out of the reconciliation, and the app says Saved. It left its reconciliation.

In a completed reconciliation: the engine locks amount, account, title and date. These are the words the app shows when you press c to complete one: Locks amount/account/title/date on the N ticked rows. Category, hashtags and note still change. A delete is refused until you revert the reconciliation to draft with u. It can be split again as long as the total stays the same, but a new title is refused. The operation number is not in that list: whether it locks is to check against the contract.

What the app does today on a completed reconciliation's row: it greys nothing out. The Edit form shows every line as changeable, and only when you save does the engine refuse, with its own words. Lines are greyed out today only on an opening balance and on a transaction of an archived account. In option A below, a locked line shows the way those do: dimmed, marked read-only, and the cursor skips it (screen A5). For that, the screen has to know the line sits in a completed reconciliation before you type, so the engine would have to say so when the app reads the line. Whether it already does is to check against the contract. If it doesn't, that becomes a request to the engine.

Today · three places for one split transaction — the ledger's ⏎, the ledger's s, and “no batch” ⏎

The ledger shows each part as its own row. From there ⏎ and s open two different screens. In Reconciliations, “no batch” shows the whole transaction as one row, and ⏎ opens a third.

Date, title and account are dimmed and marked read-only, as the Edit form shows an opening balance's locked lines, and the cursor skips them. Category, hashtags and note can still change. The operation number is drawn changeable: whether a completed reconciliation locks it is to check against the contract. The parts' amounts are drawn changeable because the engine lets such a transaction be split again while the total stays the same; this is to check against the contract too.

What one save sends — to check against the contract (the practice engine was not running, so this was not checked). The engine has two writes today. The split (POST /v1/transactions/{id}/split) carries the parts and the title. The Edit form's save (PUT /v1/transactions/{id}) on one part carries date, operation and account, and the engine writes them onto every part. A save in A that changes a part and the date, operation or account would need both writes, one after the other, and if the second were refused, half would be saved. If the split can't also take date, operation and account, the complete answer is a request to the engine: let the split take them, so one save is one write. Two more points to check: the split gives each part a new identity, so a save that only changes one part's category replaces both parts, and it is unknown whether the parts stay in their reconciliation when that happens. And A5 needs the engine to say, when the app reads the line, that it sits in a completed reconciliation.
