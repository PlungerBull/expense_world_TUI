Closing a month from the bank's PDF · the preview, the close, the statement

What follows screen A2 of expense-world-statement-pdf-upload.html (the PDF dropped on the LOG bar, its account picked). The app sends the PDF whole; the engine reads it, matches each row to a ledger line and answers what it would do, then does it on ⏎. Every word on these screens about a row comes from the engine's answer.

The example is a card statement with a soles and a dollar half; the names, amounts and file are invented. Use the button at the top right for a light terminal.

Picked

Screens 1 to 4 as drawn; 5A, an open statement on an account with a statement code waits for its PDF; 6A, several files dropped together go one by one, each its own way.

1 · The preview — nothing is written until ⏎

One line per row of the paper, in the paper's order, numbered by its place. In the ledger: the row is already a ledger line, which joins the statement. New: the ledger lacks it, and closing creates it under @Pendiente. Empty: a 0.00 row, which keeps its number and creates nothing. Lines in the statement that the paper does not print are taken out of it, and stay in the ledger.

4 · A closed statement, on Reconciliations — in the paper's order

The statement's lines in the order the paper prints them, with each line's number on the paper. On a card each half shows its own lines, so the soles half skips the dollar rows' numbers. A line with no place on the paper (an opening balance, say) is listed apart at the end. ⏎ opens a line in its Edit form.

5 · Question · an open statement on an account with a statement code — ticking can no longer close it

Today an open statement is a tick-list: space puts a line in or out, and c asks for the ending balance and completes. On an account with a statement code (your cards and BCP accounts) the engine completes nothing until every line has its place on the paper, which only the PDF gives, so ticking and c lead nowhere there. Accounts with no statement code keep the tick-list either way.
