§5 · the list panels — horizontal rules only, no vertical lines

      
The rule (owner, 2026-09-20): horizontal lines are fine and are the right way
         to divide sections; vertical ones are not. There is already precedent — the header's
         border-bottom is exactly that. So the round border every list has drawn since
         2026-07-11 goes, and the two things it absorbed need rehoming: the list's title
         (top edge) and the page status (bottom edge,
         rows 21-40 of 133 · page 2 of 7).

      
L is the labelled rule — title on the left of the divider, page status on the
         right, one row doing both jobs with no vertical edges. M splits them: a heading line,
         then a plain rule under it. K drops the label and lets the breadcrumb name the screen.

      
K breaks on the browse, and that is worth seeing before choosing. That screen
         stacks two lists, and the lower one's caption is
         Reconciliations · BCP PEN · by statement start date — the only thing on screen
         tying those batches to the account highlighted above. Drop it and two unlabelled tables sit on
         top of each other. K reads fine on the tick window, where there is one list and the crumb
         already names it; it fails where there are two. L is the one that works on both, which
         is the argument for it over a per-screen mix.

      
Scope. CursorList and CheckList share one CSS rule, so
         this lands on every list in the app at once — ledger, accounts, categories, hashtags,
         activity, rates, both reconciliation panes. Home is already borderless, so today the app has
         two styles; this converges them. Either that is the point, or it is more than should ride
         along with ask 1.
