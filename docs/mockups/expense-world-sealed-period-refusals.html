Sealed periods — what a refusal looks like

From 2026-09-20 the engine refuses writes it used to accept: a row dated inside a completed reconciliation, and a complete over a period that still holds an unticked row. Those refusals have to land somewhere a person can act on. Today every one of them is a toast that disappears.

Three sketches, three separate picks: §2 what the confirm box says before you complete, §3 what you see after the engine refuses, and §4 the results screen for a bulk operation, which you have already said yes to in principle — only its layout is open.

One fact that makes §2 nearly free

  • The draft screen already fetches and lists the account's unticked rows inside the period — they are the unchecked [ ] lines in the checklist. So the rows that will refuse the close are already on screen and already counted.
  • That means a warning costs one len(), not a round trip. It also means it can go stale if something else writes a row while the screen sits open — the engine stays the authority either way.

1 · Today — the refusal exists for two seconds

You press c, the engine refuses, and a toast slides in over the bottom-right of the screen and then goes away. Nothing names the rows, and nothing survives long enough to act on. The same shape covers all three refusal sites.

This is decision D2, which I first asked as a flat-CLI question and you correctly sent back: in the TUI the answer is not a shell command to copy, it is the rows. Both sketches keep the refusal on screen until you deal with it.

4 · The results screen for a bulk operation — layout only

You have picked this in principle: an operation that refuses rows shows them instead of a vanishing toast, appears only when something was refused, and keeps nothing between runs — the last point is what stops it becoming the Inbox again.

The case that needs it most is not reconciliation: it is selecting forty rows in Home, pressing b to move them, and having eight refused. Only the layout is open.

F · a full screen, pushed like any other

Room for every refused row with its reason, and the reason can be long — a sealed period names its month and both dates. Reads like the import preview you already know.

Q · what I need from you

1 — before completing: A say nothing, B one warning line, or C name them and offer to tick them?

B is the one I would build. It costs a len(), it cannot mislead you into an action, and it turns the most likely failure of your twenty-four closes into something you see before you commit rather than after. C is genuinely better if you find yourself ticking every listed row anyway — but it reopens a non-goal you settled on 2026-09-19, so it should be a deliberate reversal, not a side effect of this sketch.

2 — after a refusal: D banner plus flags in the list, or E the shared results screen?

D, because this screen is the one place that already holds the rows — sending you to another screen to read three rows you were already looking at is motion for consistency's sake. E's argument is real but it is about the other two sites, not this one; note that picking B above makes this case rare, since you would have been told first.

3 — the bulk results: F full screen or G modal over the ledger?

F. The reason column is the point of the screen — “which month refused this” — and G has to cut it to three letters to fit. Losing your place matters less than it sounds, because esc comes back to the ledger with the selection intact either way.

4 — does r retry belong on it?

It is drawn on both F and G and I am not sure it should be. Retrying a row a sealed period refused will be refused identically — the only thing that changes the answer is reverting the month. It is useful for a connection failure and misleading for this one. I would drop it and let ⏎ take you to the batch instead.
