Delete, from the app

Today the app can only archive an account, and deletes a transaction only from a reconciliation's “no batch” list. Deleting an account, a category, a hashtag or any other transaction needs a typed command. These screens put all four in the app, each behind the same question.

Screens 3 and 4 each have a choice to make, with both options drawn and the recommended one marked. Screens 1, 2 and 5 reuse what the app already does and have no choice. Nothing is built until you pick. The rows are invented.

1 · The key is d, on four screens — one drawing, no choice to make

d already means Delete in the two places the app deletes today (a row in a reconciliation's “no batch” list, and a reconciliation itself), and it is free on all four screens here. So: Manage ▸ Accounts, Manage ▸ Categories, Manage ▸ Hashtags and the ledger (whole, or filtered from Home) each gain d Delete at the end of the footer. It always acts on the row under the cursor, and always asks first (screen 2). a Archive on Accounts stays exactly as it is: archive puts an account away and can be undone, delete is for something made by mistake and cannot.

2 · It asks first — the confirm the app already has, one drawing each

The same question the “no batch” list asks today, laid over the list so you still see the row. It names the row, then says the one thing that matters: a delete is final. Only y deletes; ⏎, n and esc all cancel, so a reflex keypress never deletes anything.

3 · On the ledger, when rows are selected — one row at a time, or the whole selection

On the ledger you can select several rows (space, a) and the keys c b h then change all of them at once. The question is whether d follows them. With nothing selected the two options are identical: d asks about the row under the cursor (screen 2).

One question lists the rows (the first few, then “… and 9 more”) and y deletes them all. Faster for clearing duplicates after an import; a row the engine refuses is named by its number and the others still go, as with c b h today.

4 · When the engine says no — its words alone, or its words plus what to do next

The engine refuses some deletes: an account or a category that still has transactions, the two built-in system categories, and a transaction sealed inside a completed reconciliation. Nothing is deleted, the list stays as it was, and the refusal shows as a note in the bottom-right corner, as every failed save does today. The first line under Failed is always the engine's own answer. (The engine's exact words are stand-ins in these drawings until they are read from the engine.)

5 · After a delete — one drawing, no choice to make

The list is read again from the engine and starts over: first page, cursor on the first row, the deleted row gone, and a short note in the corner (Account deleted., Category deleted., Hashtag deleted., Transaction deleted.). On the ledger that means the newest ten rows again, as after any other change. There is no undo and no “recently deleted” list.
