Pendiente, not Inbox

The Inbox is deleted. A line with an account, an amount and a date is a ledger row. Category and title are free to be empty: an empty category files the row under a new system category, @Pendiente, which counts toward every total, every account balance and every reconciliation from the moment it is written. There is no second table, no promote, no "ready" state.

The quick-add grammar is untouched. What changes is the answer to "where does this line go": it goes to the ledger, or it is refused in the bar so you can fix it before anything is written.

Your three calls, 2026-09-12

  • Future dates are gone with the Inbox. The ledger keeps refusing them, and nothing else accepts them any more (pane 4).
  • An unknown #tag is refused, never created (pane 3). Same rule as 2026-08-25, now with no Inbox to fall back on.
  • A titleless row shows a dash (panes 1 and 6). Nothing is invented for it.

What the engine has to do first

  • Seed a second system category, @Pendiente (system_key = 'pending'), on the @Opening pattern: renameable, delete refused, name reserved. Unlike @Opening it is included in every flow report and can be named explicitly on a write.
  • POST /transactions and /batch: title optional, category_id optional and defaulted to @Pendiente.
  • Delete the inbox table, its six routes, promote, ?ready / ?overdue, the inbox_id back-pointer and the second hashtag junction source.

Two things this mockup leaves for you to pick

  • Pane 6 — how a pending row reads in the ledger: A the category cell alone, or B a marker column.
  • Pane 7 — where the pending count lives on Home.

6 · the ledger — two ways a pending row can read

The Inbox screen is replaced by a filter on Transactions: p cycles all → pendiente, exactly the way the Inbox's f cycled its three. The engine's ?category_id= filter already exists, so this needs nothing new from it. Finishing a row is the edit form (↵), same as any other correction. The question is how a pending row reads in the full ledger, among finished ones.

A is the recommendation. The category is the state, which is the whole point of the design: one classification, no second axis. B spends two columns on a fact the accent colour already carries, on a screen that fills the window and has fought for width twice. B earns its place only if the category column is ever hidden, and it is not.

7 · Home — the Inbox row is gone, the count is not

The menu loses its first row. The pile of unfinished rows still needs to be visible without opening the ledger, and Home's header already carries the numbers that need attention (net · spent · owed, or 4 UNRATED alone when something cannot be priced). The pending count joins that line, dim, at the end: it is a nudge, not an alarm, and it disappears at zero like owed does.

The alternative, a Pendiente menu row where Inbox was, is drawn nowhere on purpose: it would be a second door into the same ledger screen with a filter pre-set, and the Home menu lists screens, not filters. If you want it anyway, say so and it is one line in _MENU.

8 · the flat command — one line, one answer

expense log keeps its echo-then-ask. A refused line prints the reason and exits non-zero, with no prompt: there is nothing to confirm. A sparse line that is enough is echoed with the dash and the category spelled out, so you see PENDIENTE before you answer y.

zsh
$ expense log "-6.60 \$bcp pen"
  —                                                    -6.60 PEN
  BCP PEN · PENDIENTE · Friday 12 September 2026

rules · what is required now, and what is not
account · amount · dateRequired. Date defaults to today, as the grammar already does, so in practice the bar needs a signed number and a $account. Missing either: refused on ↵.
categoryOptional. Omitted → the engine files the row under @Pendiente. Naming @pendiente explicitly is allowed and means the same thing, so a row can be sent back to pending from the edit form.
title · tags · noteOptional. A missing title is stored as null and shown as — everywhere. An unknown tag is refused; a known one is attached.
a date ahead of todayRefused, on both surfaces. The engine's rule is unchanged; the bar stops offering a place to park it.
totals · balances · reconciliationA pending row is an ordinary ledger row. It is in the account balance the moment it is saved, in the monthly grid under PENDIENTE, and reconcilable like any other.
rejected · the ones that needed more than a line

Keep the Inbox but count drafts toward totals.

Every aggregate — dashboard, monthly grid, account balance, reconciliation window — would join two tables and reconcile two sign conventions, and promote would still exist to move a row that already counted. That is a ledger with a second name. One table is the smaller change and the more honest one.

A pending flag on the row instead of a category.

A second axis every filter and every client has to know about, and it permits a contradiction — a row with a real category and the flag set. "Unclassified" is a classification; @Opening already proved the engine can carry a meaning on a category, and the reports keyed off system_key need no new concept.

Default a missing title to the category or account name.

Invented text that reads exactly like something you typed, and that survives into every export forever. A dash is the one rendering that cannot be mistaken for data.
