Putting a statement in order

A statement line such as SIG-202603-007 is made by the engine from three things: the account's statement code (SIG), the month the reconciliation ends (March 2026), and the line's place on the paper statement (7). This new screen is where you give each line its place, once per statement, by copying the order of the paper.

A card statement is two reconciliations over the same days, one in soles and one in dollars, and the paper mixes them. So this screen shows both currencies in one list, and either half opens the same list. Where the paper has a line the ledger doesn't have (a fee of 0.00, say), you leave an empty slot, so the next line keeps the paper's number.

What you arrange here stays on the screen until you save it with ctrl+s: the whole order is one save, and the engine takes it only when every line has its place. A reconciliation on an account with a statement code can't be completed until its statement is in order; the statement lines then show on its lines.

The card, its lines and amounts are invented: Signature Soles (PEN) and Signature Dólares (USD), both with the code SIG, and their reconciliations Marzo 2026, 2026-02-21 → 2026-03-20. The engine's refusals are its real words, shown whole as every refusal in the app is. The lines under them, marked as the app's, are proposals, yours to change. Where the contract gives only a refusal's field words, its general first line (Validation failed., CONFLICT — …) is drawn as earlier screens show it. Use the button at the top right for a light terminal.

1 · Arranging the lines — one list, both currencies · three ways to choose from

The screen has two parts. On top, the order: each line with its place (001, 002 … as it will read at the end of its statement line) and its currency (Cur), then the bank's own columns as on a reconciliation today. Below, “no place yet”: the lines that have no place, in the engine's order, newest first. The first time you open a statement every line is down there; a line added to the reconciliation later shows up there too. Drawn mid-way: places 001 to 006 done (004 is an empty slot), four lines to go. The question is how a line gets its place.

2 · Empty slots — a line on the paper that the ledger doesn't have

e puts an empty slot above the cursor line, and every line under it moves down one number. With the cursor under “no place yet”, it goes at the end of the order instead, so reading the paper top to bottom you press e exactly where the paper has the line you don't. x on an empty slot removes it, and the lines under it move up one; x shows in the footer only on an empty slot. An empty slot can be moved with shift+↑↓ like a line. Drawn with 1 A; the question is how an empty slot looks.

3 · Saving the order, and when the engine refuses — drawn with 1 A and 2 A

ctrl+s sends the whole order in one save, every line and every empty slot, both currencies. Nothing is sent before that. The line under the list says whether what you see is saved. A saved statement can be opened and changed again any time while both halves are drafts; a line added to either half later shows under “no place yet” until you place it and save again.
