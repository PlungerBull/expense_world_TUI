Where the statement line shows

A statement line such as SIG-202609-009 is a bank line's row on the paper statement: the account's statement code (SIG), the statement's month (202609, the month its reconciliation ends) and its place on the paper (009). The engine makes it. Nobody types it. A bank line shows one only once its reconciliation is completed. Before that, and always on an account with no statement code (Efectivo, a person), it is empty. You want to see it so you can match each line with its row on the paper.

Today no screen shows a statement line. The ledger, a reconciliation's lines and the Edit form have none. Three places to choose: the ledger (1), a reconciliation's lines (2) and the Edit form (3). Every screen keeps its real layout and words, and only the statement line is new. The accounts and rows are invented. The card is Signature PEN and Signature USD, both with the code SIG. Its September statement (2026-08-26 → 2026-09-25) is completed, and its October one is still a draft. Sueldo Soles has the code PEN, and Efectivo has none. Use the button at the top right to see the page on a light terminal.

The “no batch” list and its Edit form stay as they are: a line in no reconciliation never has a statement line.

1 · The ledger — drawn 100 columns wide by the app's own table

The ledger mixes every account and month, newest first. The four October rows are in a draft reconciliation, or on Efectivo, so they have no statement line. The September rows are in completed ones, so they have one. Both parts of a split transaction (TOTTUS JOCKEY) read the same statement line, because it belongs to the bank line, not to the part. Any change of columns needs your sign-off, so B shows the full set of columns it proposes. The tables are drawn by the app's own code at 100 columns, so the cut words are where the app would really cut them.

2 · A reconciliation's lines — one half of the card, Signature PEN

The reconciliation's screen keeps its columns (date, title, operation number, amount) and gains one at the end. Each option is drawn twice: October, still a draft and already put in order once, then September, completed. In October the soles lines hold places 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6. Place 3 is a dollar line, in the card's other half, because the paper statement mixes both currencies. CINE PLAZA was ticked after the order was sent, so it has no place yet, and RAPPI is not in the reconciliation. The rows stay in the engine's order, newest first, as today. The paper's order is the job of the screen that puts a statement in order, drawn on its own.

Pick:
  • 1 · The ledger: A no change, B a new last column Statement line, or C only for the row under the cursor, under the list. Recommended: C. At 100 columns a new column cuts titles to 7 letters, and the ledger is where you sort rows by their titles. Going down a statement line by line against the paper happens on a reconciliation's lines (2), which show every code of one statement in one list. C still tells you the code of any ledger row you land on. If you would rather see every code in the ledger and accept the short titles, pick B.
  • 2 · A reconciliation's lines: A Statement line only, empty on a draft, or B Place on a draft and Statement line once completed. Recommended: B. On a draft you see which ticked lines still have no place, and completing is refused for those, before you press c. Once completed, A and B are the same.
  • 3 · The Edit form: A a read-only line under account, B a dim line over the bar, or C a dim line under the fields. Recommended: A. It reads like the read-only lines you already know from opening balances: dimmed, marked read-only, and skipped by ⇥.
  • 3b · The Edit form with none yet: A shows — read-only, or B leaves the line off. Recommended: A. The form is the same on every row, and an empty statement line is plain to see.
