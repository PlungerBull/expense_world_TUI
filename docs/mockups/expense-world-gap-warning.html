Gap warning — what step 3 looks like

Ask 1, step 3 (sealed-periods-plan.md §3 and D1). When you complete a reconciliation, the engine checks the previous completed one on the same account. If there are days in between that no completed period covers, the "OK" comes back with a warning attached. The flat CLI prints it since 2026-09-21. The TUI throws it away.

The code change underneath is the same whichever way you pick: the TUI's write helper learns to hand the engine's reply to the screen. What's open is only what you see. The plan said "a toast"; the flow mockup of 2026-09-20 drew a full screen; neither was picked. A toast, B a line on the screen, C a full screen — then D and E, which answer the owner's question of 2026-09-22: why name the dates instead of showing the rows that are actually sitting in those days?

Settled 2026-09-22 — pick B

  • B is built (§B below): a line under the header, in the warning colour, holding the engine's own sentence. It does not fade, it survives r, and it clears on the next status change. The "completed" toast still fires.
  • D and E are dropped, and with them the engine-side ask. They were the better answer to "show me the rows" but needed the gap's dates as fields. The need behind them — review everything unreconciled, spot rows from periods long closed — is served by transactions list --unreconciled today and becomes a standing view of its own in the TUI (its own item in todo.md), not a by-product of closing a period.
  • Why, with every rejected option: decisions.md "The gap report is a line under the header, not a toast and not the rows".

The example used in every pane

  • Account BCP PEN. June 2026 (05-26 → 06-25) is completed. July was skipped. You're completing August 2026 (07-24 → 08-25).
  • So 06-26 → 07-23 belongs to no completed period. A stray or mistyped row dated in those days is never checked by anything: the close check only looks inside a period, and the diff figure only adds up the rows you ticked.
  • The warning text is the engine's own wording, shown as-is (the "engine errors surface cleanly" rule: never reworded, never trimmed).
  • When there's no gap, all three options look exactly like today.

One snag in D and E — and it's the engine's to fix

  • To fetch the rows, this side needs the gap's two dates as data. Today the engine sends the gap as one English sentence: warnings is a list of strings.
  • Reading the dates out of that sentence is out of the question — prose is not a contract. The engine rewords the message one day and this side silently stops finding rows, or finds them for the wrong days.
  • Working the gap out here instead — fetch the account's batches, find the last completed one before this window — means copying an engine rule into the client, which is the one thing this repo never does. It would also drift: the engine's rule counts only completed, dated periods, in the account's timezone.
  • So D and E need one engine-side ask: carry the gap as fields, not only prose — its first and last uncovered day, beside the message. The count could come with it (the engine has the same query one statement away), which would save this side a read. This is the CLI doing its job: the warning is currently unactionable by any client, and the web and iOS clients would have hit the identical wall.
  • A, B and C need nothing from the engine and can ship today. D and E can also ship in two steps: the line now, the count and the door when the fields land.

What to pick, and one thing I noticed

Which one: A, B, C, D or E? — answered: B (2026-09-22)

My lean had been D — the rows are the real answer, and the count is what tells an alarming gap from an empty one. What settled it the other way: D needs the engine to send the gap as fields, and the review it would have opened is wanted across all time, not one stretch at a time. So: B here, and a standing unreconciled view as its own item. A fades, so it can be missed; C and E cost a keypress on every gap, including the deliberate ones.

Which rows count as "in the gap"?

I drew rows in no reconciliation at all, matching the existing orphan check. A row in the gap that belongs to a draft batch is at least accounted for by someone. The other reading — every row in those days, filed or not — makes the count bigger and less pointed. Say which you want.

Shall I raise the engine ask? — answered: no (2026-09-22)

It would have been the first open engine-side ask since 2026-09-20: put the gap's two dates on the response as fields, plus the count of unfiled rows in it. Dropped with D and E. It stays written down here in case a client ever needs to act on the warning rather than show it — the sentence is still the only place those dates exist.

The engine's dates are the fences, not the gap

The engine's message says 2026-06-25 to 2026-07-24: that's June's last covered day and August's first day. The days actually uncovered are 06-26 → 07-23. Read quickly, it looks like a period that includes 06-25 and 07-24. The CLI must show the text as-is, so if you want it reworded, that's a note for the engine side (for example "the days between 2026-06-25 and 2026-07-24"). It doesn't block this step.
