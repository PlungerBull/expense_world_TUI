Split · one purchase into parts

One bank charge, several things: -100.00 at Tottus can be -60.00 of food and -40.00 of cleaning products. Splitting turns the one ledger row into parts, each with its own amount, category, hashtags and note; together they still are the one bank charge, so a later bank upload still finds it. How the ledger shows the parts is in its own mockup.

The rows are invented. Use the button at the top right to see the page on a light terminal.

1 · How you get there — s on a ledger row

On the ledger, s Split opens the split screen for the row under the cursor, whole ledger or filtered from Home. Like d, it works on the cursor row only: while rows are selected it is off and leaves the footer. On a purchase already in parts, s on any of its parts opens the same screen with all its parts (screen 3).

2 · Splitting a purchase — Tottus Jockey Plaza, -100.00, into two parts

A full screen. On top, the purchase as the ledger has it: its title, date and account belong to the whole purchase, so every part keeps them and they are not asked here. Each part has its own amount, category, hashtags and note. A part with no category goes to @Pendiente, and one with no hashtag to #Pendiente, as anywhere else. ^s sends every part at once, and the engine checks that they add up to the purchase: the app never stops you from sending. Here part 1 (-60.00, COMIDA, #casa, verduras) is in, and you are typing part 2.

Part 1 opens holding -100.00, and every part you add is taken from it, so the total always matches. Fewer refusals, but the app does the subtraction for you, and part 1's amount changes without you typing it.

In B and C the line takes no $account and no date: typing one is refused in place (“a part keeps the purchase's account and date”), the way the LOG bar refuses a line it cannot stage. A mixed purchase is allowed: -120.00 out and +20.00 in make -100.00.

3 · Splitting it again — s on a part of a split purchase

s on either part of Tottus opens the same screen with its current parts already in the list. You change them, drop one or add one, and ^s sends the whole new set: say -70.00 and -30.00 instead of -60.00 and -40.00. The same screen splits one part further, by adding parts. 4 · The parts do not add up — SPLIT_TOTAL_MISMATCH

You changed part 2 to -30.00 and pressed ^s. The parts make -90.00, the purchase is -100.00. The engine refuses and writes nothing; its sentence gives both totals in cents, and it names the parts as well as each part's amount. The engine's sentence is its real one; the first line is a stand-in. The screen stays as it was, so you fix a part and press ^s again.
