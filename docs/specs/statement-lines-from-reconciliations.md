## What changed

**A statement line is generated, never typed.** A statement line (`SIG-202603-001`, the sheet's
`COD_EECC`) is no longer typed or sent by anyone. The engine builds it from three things:

- **The account's statement code**: three capital letters for the bank product (`SIG`, `PEN`,
  `USD`, `ORO`), set on the account. A card's soles and dollar accounts share one code; a person
  never has one.
- **The statement's month**: the month its reconciliation ends. One statement per code per
  month. A card statement is two reconciliations, one per currency, over exactly the same days.
- **The line's place on the paper statement**: the owner puts the statement's lines in the
  paper's order once, both currencies mixed (contract: PUT /v1/reconciliations/{reconciliation_id}/statement-order).

A line shows its statement line only once its reconciliation is completed. Completing a
reconciliation on an account with a code is refused until every line of the statement has a
place (contract: POST /v1/reconciliations/{reconciliation_id}/complete). Every statement line
stored before is gone.

**One write edits a whole bank line.** A line's fields (title, date, account, operation code,
reconciliation) and its complete set of parts go in one write; a kept part keeps its id, a new id
makes a new part, and a part left out is deleted (contract: PUT /v1/bank-lines/{bank_line_id}).
The split route is gone.

**A statement is read in the paper's order.** The statement order lists the lines with no place
oldest first, both currencies mixed by date (contract: GET /v1/reconciliations/{reconciliation_id}/statement-order).
A reconciliation's lines can be listed in the statement's order: placed lines by place, then the
rest (contract: GET /v1/bank-lines). A transaction can be created straight into a
reconciliation, in one write (contract: POST /v1/transactions).

**A bank line says when it is its account's opening balance.** Every bank line carries
`is_opening_balance`, always `true` or `false`; the opening balance is the line a statement's
order never places (contract: GET /v1/bank-lines/{bank_line_id}, "Response fields").

**The engine lists the lines a statement still needs.** The bank-line list, and its count, take
a reconciliation's id and list the lines its statement still needs: on a card both halves' lines
mixed, oldest first in the statement's order, exactly the lines a complete would count as
unassigned, so the list is empty exactly when the complete would not refuse for them
(contract: GET /v1/bank-lines, "Filters").

**A card statement is one statement.** Every reconciliation names its other half and says how
far its statement is from ordered: the lines with no place, and the places held twice; both at
zero means it can complete (contract: GET /v1/reconciliations). The list reads a whole card from
either card account, both halves of every month in one answer, each item's other half among the
same items (contract: GET /v1/reconciliations). One create makes both halves
(contract: POST /v1/reconciliations). Complete, revert, delete, rename and moving the days act on both halves in
one write, from either half; there is no way left to act on one half of a card alone
(contract: POST /v1/reconciliations/{reconciliation_id}/complete) (contract: DELETE /v1/reconciliations/{reconciliation_id}) (contract: PUT /v1/reconciliations/{reconciliation_id}).
One save of a half also writes the other half's balances
(contract: PUT /v1/reconciliations/{reconciliation_id}). Until task 7 ships, the app's complete, revert and
delete on one half of a card already act on both, while its screens show one half.

## What the engine settled

It answers eight asks of this client's, all raised 2026-10-24: "One write edits a whole bank
line: its line fields and its full set of parts", "The statement order lists a statement's
unplaced lines oldest first", "A reconciliation's bank lines can be listed in the statement's
order", "A transaction can be created straight into a reconciliation", "A reconciliation names
its statement's other half and whether the statement is ordered", "One write completes or
reverts a whole statement, both halves of a card", "One write creates, or deletes, both halves of
a card statement" and "PUT on either half of a card moves the name and the dates on both". And
four raised 2026-10-25: "A bank line says when it is an account's opening balance", "The
statement read lists every line a statement needs, on any account, oldest first", "The
reconciliation list reads a card statement as one row" and "Saving a card statement's form
writes both halves' balances in one write". Where it differs from what was asked:

- **The split route is removed** (the owner's decision): the whole-line write is the one way to
  rewrite a line's parts, with every rule the split had.
- **The whole-line write takes no statement line or place.** No client sends either.
- **The statement sort on bank lines** is taken only with a reconciliation, or with the lines a
  statement still needs; the count takes no sort.
- **The parts of one batch line group** name the same reconciliation or none.
- **A card statement's create refuses one check at a time**, not all in one answer.
- **The lines a statement still needs come from the bank-line list**, not from the statement
  read: the list takes the reconciliation's id, on any account, coded or not. The statement
  order's write does not answer with them, so the screen reads the list again after a write.
- **The statement read on an account with no statement code: declined.** It stays refused
  there; that account's lines, oldest first, come from the bank-line list in the statement's
  order, so a second read would add nothing. The screen makes two reads and sorts nothing: the
  statement (the statement read on a coded account, the bank-line list in the statement's order
  on an uncoded one) and the lines still to tick.
- **The other half's status, difference and ending balance on each list item: declined.** A
  reference carries only the other half's id; on a whole card's list that id always names
  another item of the same answer, so the row reads the other half's fields from there, with no
  second read.
- **The other half's balances on a save name the other half by its id,** the one the client
  read: a stale id is refused as a concurrent change (re-read and save again), a half with no
  other half refuses them, and an other-half part with no balance in it is refused. When the
  other half's balances did not change, the save leaves them out.

## What the owner settled

- **The sheet's `COD_EECC` is ignored at the official import** (owner, 2026-10-24): it only
  groups split rows, as the import already does, and an imported line's statement line stays
  empty until its month is reconciled and ordered in the app ([decisions.md](../decisions.md)
  "The sheet import brings a split purchase in as one split transaction"). No task follows from
  it.
- **The order screen creates nothing and has no empty slots** (owner, 2026-10-24, kept
  2026-10-25): a paper line the ledger lacks comes in from the bank's file through the import
  door. So the engine's "Create the missing line here" and "insert empty slots" get no screen;
  the live contract suite proves both writes.
- **The statement line is never shown** (owner, 2026-10-24, kept 2026-10-25): it only keeps a
  statement's lines in the paper's order. So the engine's "Show `statement_line`" gets no screen.

## Found along the way

Not built in this spec; drafted into a new spec when it is done.

- **A reconciliation's lines ticked for you** (owner, 2026-10-24): an automatic way to choose
  which lines belong to a month, e.g. every line dated inside the statement's days, instead of
  ticking each; an engine ask. It also covers the lines a bank file brings in: the owner wants a
  paper line the ledger lacks to reach its statement with no step by hand.
- **No unit guard refuses `statement_line` or `statement_place` in a request body**, as
  `_no_line_code_in_requests` in [tests/unit/conftest.py](../../tests/unit/conftest.py) does for
  `line_code`.
- **[test_cmd_import.py](../../tests/unit/test_cmd_import.py) copies one account record about 25
  times**; one small builder would do.
- **The complete answer's unit fixtures lack `coverage_gap`**, which the contract requires on
  that answer (contract: POST /v1/reconciliations/{reconciliation_id}/complete); the drift check
  catches only fields the engine does not serve, never a required one a fixture leaves out.
- **`FakeClient` answers every write with a bare `{}`**, so the reconciliation create, revert,
  delete and some completes in the unit tests get no `twin`; nothing reads those answers today.
- **Copies in the contract suite.** `_live_ids` sits in four contract test files, `_send` in
  [test_card_statement.py](../../tests/contract/test_card_statement.py) and
  test_statement_order.py; `_batch_into` in
  [test_statement_order.py](../../tests/contract/test_statement_order.py) repeats `Scratch.line`
  in [conftest.py](../../tests/contract/conftest.py). And the two guards that nothing names the
  split route, over `expense/` and `tests/contract/` in
  [test_bank_line_write.py](../../tests/unit/test_bank_line_write.py), could be one over the
  whole repo, whose wording says "the removed split route" where the present tense would say
  the contract holds none.
- **Two card-statement answers the live suite does not pin**: a complete on a card whose halves
  are both completed already, which writes nothing, and `UNASSIGNED_IN_PERIOD` on both halves at
  once (contract: POST /v1/reconciliations/{reconciliation_id}/complete, "Validation").
- **A statement holding the opening balance must begin at 0.00.** The engine adds an assigned
  opening balance into the statement's sum, so typing the paper's starting figure as the begin
  balance too counts it twice; the create form says nothing of it today.
- **The account read does not name a card's other account.** The create form finds a card's
  partner by grouping accounts on their statement code, dropping people and codes not held by

  engine ask, spares every client the grouping.
