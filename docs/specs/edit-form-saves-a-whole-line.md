## What changed

**One write edits a whole bank line.** A line's fields (title, date, account, operation code,
reconciliation) and its complete set of parts go in one write; a kept part keeps its id, a new id
makes a new part, and a part left out is deleted (contract: PUT /v1/bank-lines/{bank_line_id}).

**That write takes every part edit a transaction's own write takes**, on the two kinds of line
where it refused `parts` whole (contract: PUT /v1/bank-lines/{bank_line_id}, "The parts' rules"):

- **A line holding an opening balance:** `parts` names exactly the line's parts; its amount and
  note change under the usual rules; its category may be sent only as it is (`@Opening`), its
  hashtags only as none.
- **A line with a part on an archived account:** `parts` names exactly the line's parts; each
  part on the archived account is sent its current amount; its category, hashtags and note
  change under the usual rules.

A refusal of a kept part's field lands on that part's field (`parts.<i>.amount_cents`,
`parts.<i>.category_id`, `parts.<i>.hashtag_ids`); a part added or left out is refused on
`parts`.

**Every bank line says what a write would refuse on it.** Each line the engine returns carries
`edit_limits`: the line fields a write would refuse, each with the reason the write gives;
whether the total is fixed; whether the parts are fixed (none added, none dropped); and, per
part, the part fields a write would refuse, each with its reason. It is worked out on every read
by the write's own rules, and a revert or an unarchive clears what it closed on the next read
(contract: GET /v1/bank-lines/{bank_line_id}, "Response fields"). A transaction's read does not
carry it.

## What it must do

From the engine's "What the TUI does":

1. **Catch up with the contract:** `edit_limits` on every bank line, and `parts` on an opening
   balance's line and an archived account's line, with `OPENING_BALANCE_CATEGORY_FIXED` and
   `OPENING_BALANCE_NOT_TAGGED`.
2. **The line editor saves with one write:** line fields and the full set of parts together,
   sending a kept part's hashtags only when the owner changed them, and no line field on a line
   in a completed reconciliation unless the owner changed it.
3. **Every save goes through that write,** an opening balance's line and an archived account's
   line included: no switch to a transaction's own write for those lines, and no chain of one
   write per part. Each kept part's current amount is sent as it is; a refusal shows on the
   field its key names.
4. **The line editor reads `edit_limits`** on the line it opens: every field it names is
   read-only, adding and dropping parts is off when the parts are fixed, and the total is kept
   when it is fixed. No reconciliation read on opening a line, and no copy of the locking rules
   in the app.

## What the engine settled

It answers two asks of this client's, both raised 2026-10-25: "The whole-line write takes every
part edit that PUT /transactions/{id} takes" and "A bank line's read says which of its fields a
write would refuse". Where it differs from what was asked, or settles what the asks left open:

- **A kept part's hashtags always count as a change** when sent, even unchanged, so the client
  leaves them out of a part whose hashtags the owner did not touch. A field left out of a kept
  part stays as it is.
- **On an archived account, a part's amount equal to its current one is not a change**, so the
  client sends each part's amount as it is. The same size in the other direction is a change. A
  transaction's own write keeps its stricter rule: there an amount sent on an archived account is
  refused even at its current value.
- **An opening balance's part may be sent its current category**; any other is refused.
- **`edit_limits` is on bank lines only**, not on a transaction's read. It names only what the
  line's state closes: a refusal that depends on the value sent (a future date, a taken
  operation code, a move onto an archived account) stays the write's.

## Found along the way

Not built in this spec; drafted into a new spec when it is done.

- **Splitting a line that was never split replaces its one transaction.** The Split screen opens
  empty on such a line and its save leaves that transaction out, so the engine deletes it and the
  new parts take fresh ids, as the removed split route did. The whole-line write can keep it as
  the first part, with its id and history (contract: PUT /v1/bank-lines/{bank_line_id}, "Parts");
  whether to is the owner's call.
- **The Split screen keeps two paths the contract rules out**: a row with no `bank_line_id`
  (it would send `PUT /bank-lines/None`) and a part with no `id` dropped from the table (the save
  would then delete it), in [split.py](../../expense/tui/screens/split.py); the contract requires
  both fields, so both paths can go.
- **Two "did the hashtags change" checks.** The Edit form compares lists in order with the
  pending hashtag alone (`edit_form.py`), `kept_part` in
  [bank_line_write.py](../../expense/bank_line_write.py) compares sets; and `kept_part` takes a
  part's raw hashtags while `new_part` takes them filtered. Task 2 moving the Edit form onto
  `kept_part` leaves one check.
